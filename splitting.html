Splitting channel and contour detection

With execution_mode='parallel' or 'mpi' channels and contours are detected one raster per task. Detection is the costly part of these refinements on a large DEM, so a raster much larger (or busier) than the others keeps one worker busy while the rest wait. split_size detects on windows of such rasters instead, as separate tasks. It is off by default.

import ocsmesh

hfun = ocsmesh.Hfun(
    tile_paths, hmin=50, hmax=3000, method='exact',
    execution_mode='mpi',
    split_size=4000)          # windows of at most ~4000 x 4000 pixels
hfun.add_channel(level=0, width=2000, target_size=500,
                 resampling_factor=0.125)

Only the detection is split. The size function of every raster is computed whole, from the shapes detected on all its windows, exactly as without splitting, so depth bands, flow limiters, constraints and their rates, patches and line features are not affected.

split_size counts pixels of each DEM at its own resolution, whatever resolution the shapes are detected on. Rasters no larger than split_size are detected whole. Splitting applies to the 'exact' method.

Windows, halo and core

The windows of a raster are cut from its detection grid: the DEM at the resolution the definition is detected on (its own resampling_factor), laid out for the whole raster. Every window pixel is therefore a pixel of the whole raster’s grid; resampling each window on its own would shift its pixels.

Each window is a core plus a halo around it, and only the shapes in the core are kept. The cores of a raster tile its grid, so every pixel is detected once. The halo gives the core the neighbourhood detection looks at: a channel is only recognised when its whole width is visible, and without a halo the window edges themselves look like channels (a water body cut by an edge leaves narrow strips that the detection keeps). Its width, split_halo in metres, is the channel width plus its simplification tolerance and four pixels when not given; contours only need the four pixels.

The shapes of all windows are then put together on the coordinator:

  • channel detection drops channels smaller than width squared minus a quarter circle (0.86 km² for a 2 km width). That is decided on the whole channel, not on the window: a creek network cut by a window edge can leave pieces below that size whose channel is not. Windows keep their small pieces, and the pieces that continue each other across the edge between two cores are joined before the cutoff is applied;

  • contour pieces are merged back into whole lines, so that they are sampled along their length (add_feature) as unsplit lines are.

Choosing split_size

A raster W pixels wide is cut into ceil(W / split_size) equal parts along each axis, and the same along y, so no window is larger than split_size and rasters no larger than it are detected whole. With tiles of about 8,100 pixels (0.25 degree at 3 m), any value from 4,050 to 8,099 gives 2 x 2 windows, 2,700 to 4,049 gives 3 x 3.

split_size='optimum' uses the size found on the New Jersey test domain, 4100 (2 x 2 windows of its 3 m tiles; channels detected 2.2 times faster than unsplit with MPI, with the same result), and warns that other domains or DEMs may need another value.

Splitting helps when there are fewer rasters than workers, or when one raster takes much longer than the others, and only when detection is a large part of the run. The halo is detected for every window and then thrown away, so a window should be several times wider than its halo: with a 2 km channel width and 3 m pixels, 4000 pixel (12 km) cores are detected on 16 km windows, 1.8 times the pixels of the core. Smaller windows also have more edges, where the pieces of channels have to be joined.

Results match detection on whole rasters up to one pixel along the edges of detected channels: their polygons lie on pixel edges, and whether a pixel next to one counts as touched can change with rounding.
